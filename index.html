Clothesline math

Line drying is free, but it punishes the unprepared: a load that outgrows the line, a pin basket that runs dry at the last sock, an evening load that is still damp at breakfast. Clothesline math turns the load into centimeters of line and a pin count with spares, and the day's weather into drying hours on a labeled scale. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Line plan - items at a labeled spacing each become the centimeters of line needed, and the slack against the line you have earns a fit verdict.
  • Pin plan - items times pins each become the pin count, plus a labeled 10% for the ones that vanish.
  • Dry plan - temperature and humidity become drying hours on a labeled scale from quick to overnight.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 12 cm of line an item, 2 pins an item, 10% spares, and a drying base of 6 hours at 20 C and 50% humidity - all illustrative, since fabric weight, wind and shade move every number. Thick cotton takes longer than the scale says; trust the touch test, not the clock.
